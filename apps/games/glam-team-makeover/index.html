Method & sources

Clinician reference — not shown to the learner

Turn-taking taught as a short behavior chain — take a turn → relinquish → wait → ask for it back → resume — inside a motivating shared activity (a collaborative glam makeover). Both players take the same-size turn; the learner earns extra time as a reward for handing over.

Relinquish & wait = tolerance for delayed reinforcement
Handing over is reinforced (a token); "earn more time" fades the delay in reverse. Fisher, Thompson, Hagopian, Bowman & Krug (2000), Behavior Modification.
"They forget" → I ask = contrived MO + interrupted chain
Withholding the expected turn contrives a motivating operation, evoking the mand under MO control. Sundberg et al. (2002); Hall & Sundberg (1987); Endicott & Higbee (2007).
Fadeable visual cue > vocal prompt
The ask-cue steps down (full → short → icon → none) so responding transfers to the MO, not the prompt. Sweeney-Kerwin et al. (2007).
Turn-taking as a social-communication target
Structured back-and-forth with visual "my turn / their turn" supports and reinforcement (Lee & Staggs, 2021; Schertz et al., 2018).
