# BT session note rulings

Kaleb's rulings on what a submission-worthy BT session note is. From his marks
on six example notes (4 Oct 2026, the "BT Note Examples" review) and his
handout, BT Session Note Resources. The bench's BT checks
(`scripts/bench/cases/bt.json`) and the BT prompt work cite this file; quotes
are his.

## The bar

- He marked a note that was formal and accurate but generic "juuust shy of
  acceptable". A note with subjective status, vague adjectives and fragments is
  "not borderline. deficient." The bar is higher than "nothing false".
- Great looked like this: every guide point met, progress told as prompting and
  mastery rather than ReThink's numbers, effects stated, picks right. He liked
  lesson progress laid out one program per paragraph ("Program: details"). Plain
  in tone is acceptable, "a style some aim for".

## Section by section

**Client status.** Observable and "rooted in a BEHAVIOR", with the evidence
("appeared tired as evidenced by yawning"). A word like "perked up" needs "a sign
or two". Do not name someone who was not there, or not involved, without a
reason: "Why mention someone NOT there?"

**Lesson progress.**
- Every program mentioned has its result. "Worked on manding for help" with no
  result is a fault.
- A BT never masters a target: "mastering is not for a technician to say. They
  can say 'at mastery criteria'".
- A program not run is left out, unless it is a recurring barrier the BCBA should
  address, which goes in Concerns.
- Qualitative: how the trials went, responses in context, what did or did not go
  well. ReThink carries the numbers (the handout), and a note that is "a lot of
  numbers rather than responses in context" falls short.

**Antecedent modifications.**
- The checklist names the strategies; the narrative says how each was put in
  place and when ("before or after behaviors?").
- What happened at a transition is behavior, so it goes under Behavior & Staff
  Response.
- Never a consequence listed as an antecedent.

**Consequence strategies and behavior.**
- Each strategy described as implemented, a sentence each.
- The sequence: what the BT did, specifically ("how do you block a flop?").
- Whether the client followed the prompt or the BT modeled it.
- What "helped" means: "Less recurrence? Lower intensity/shorter than normal?"
- "No aggression occurred", not "was observed".
- Reinforcement is contingent on behavior, never on a person. A note must not read
  as reinforcing a tantrum: reinforce precursors or lower levels, redirect toward
  communication, with differentially better reinforcement for those.

**Effectiveness.** One of the three options, always.

**Client progress.** Progress, not performance, and "steady" needs a benchmark or
recent performance to stand against. Where the note does not support steady, he
"would probably land on interpreting this as moderate progress". (Open question
to him, 4 Oct: the tool's BT options have no "moderate".)

**Concerns / questions.** Direct, formal, numbered BT actions: "BT to follow up
with BCBA about 1. Request from parent about toilet training program goal 2.
Replace worn / missing pieces velcro on the token board".

## Words that fail

"responded well", "did great", "went well", "supported [the client] throughout",
"helped" (without the effect), "mastered", "was observed" (for something that did
not occur), "was reinforced" (of a person). He called this kind of language
"painting with white on white canvas".
